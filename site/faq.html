Getting started

What does ClipVault do?

It watches the Twitch streamers you choose and saves their best moments to a folder on your computer: the clips viewers made, plus moments nobody clipped that it finds from chat. It also makes a vertical, phone-ready version of each one with a headline and captions.

Is ClipVault free?

[YOUR ANSWER, for example: Yes. ClipVault is free to download and use.]

Which computers does it run on?

Windows 10 and 11 (64-bit), and Macs with Apple silicon (M1 or newer). Linux and a Docker image for an always-on server are coming. Once it’s running, you can open its control panel on your phone over the same Wi-Fi.

Do I need a Twitch account?

Yes. Sign in with your Twitch account during setup: ClipVault shows a short code that you enter at twitch.tv/activate. ClipVault only reads; it never posts or writes in chat.

What else do I need to install?

Nothing. The video tools ClipVault uses (FFmpeg, yt-dlp and TwitchDownloaderCLI) come inside the download. The speech model for subtitles, about 500 MB, downloads by itself the first time ClipVault makes subtitles.

Everyday use

Do I have to keep ClipVault open?

It needs to be running to find new clips, so it starts when you log in to your computer (you can switch that off in Settings). It sits next to the clock on Windows and in the menu bar on a Mac, and checks every 30 minutes. If your computer sleeps or is switched off, it catches up when it’s back.

Will it drain my laptop battery?

On battery it keeps downloading, but waits for the charger before heavier work like subtitles and phone versions. You can change this in Settings.

How much disk space does it use?

The app itself takes about 700 MB. Clips take roughly 10 GB a month for each streamer, mostly phone versions. Clips older than 60 days with fewer views than usual are cleaned up automatically. Starred clips and anything in the Favorites folder are always kept. Downloads pause if your disk gets low.

Can I keep my library on an external drive?

Yes. Choose the folder in Settings. If the drive is unplugged, new clips wait on your computer and move over when it’s back.

How do I get notifications on my phone?

Install the free ntfy app and scan the code shown in setup or Settings. You’ll get one summary each morning, plus a message only when something needs you. Quiet hours hold messages overnight.

Your clips and the rules

Does ClipVault post to TikTok or YouTube?

No. It saves clips and phone-ready versions to your computer and doesn’t upload or post anything.

Can I share or repost the clips I save?

The clips belong to the streamers who made them, and the music or game footage in them may belong to others. ClipVault is for your own archive. Before you share a clip anywhere, get the streamer’s permission and follow the rules of the site you post on. This isn’t legal advice.

Is this allowed by Twitch?

ClipVault finds clips through Twitch’s official API. Some features use other open-source tools to fetch video and chat. You’re responsible for using it in line with Twitch’s Terms of Service and the law where you live.

Some saved clips have no sound. Why?

Twitch mutes copyrighted music in stream recordings. ClipVault keeps those moments and marks them as muted.

Privacy and security

Does ClipVault send my data anywhere?

Your clips and library stay on your computer, and nothing is sent to us. ClipVault talks to Twitch to find and fetch clips, checks GitHub once a day for a newer download tool, and downloads the speech model once. Optional features send data only to the services you set up: transcription and AI headlines (if you add those keys) and ntfy (if you turn on phone notifications). The Privacy page lists each one.

Where are my keys stored?

Your Twitch sign-in, your panel password and any keys you add are kept in your operating system’s keychain, not in a file. Keys are never shown again after you save them.

Who can open the control panel?

By default only your computer. If you allow phones on your Wi-Fi, the panel is still protected by the password you chose.

Troubleshooting

My computer warns me when I open the installer.

ClipVault isn’t code-signed yet (signing certificates cost money), so Windows and macOS warn you the first time.

Windows: if you see “Windows protected your PC”, choose More info, then Run anyway.

Mac: open ClipVault from Applications. When macOS says it can’t check the app, choose Done, then open System Settings › Privacy & Security, scroll down and choose Open Anyway.

To check first that your download is the real one, compare its SHA-256 checksum with the one listed under the download buttons. On Windows, run certutil -hashfile ClipVault-Setup-Windows.exe SHA256 in Command Prompt; on a Mac, run shasum -a 256 ClipVault-macOS.dmg in Terminal.

Downloads stopped working.

Twitch changes things now and then. The download tool updates itself once a day, so this usually fixes itself. If it doesn’t, the Home screen shows a Fix button.

How do I update ClipVault?

Download the newest version from this site and install it over the old one; on a Mac, replace the app in Applications. Your library, settings and Twitch sign-in are kept.

How do I uninstall it and remove my data?

Windows: Settings › Apps › ClipVault › Uninstall. Mac: switch off “Open when I log in” in ClipVault’s Settings, quit it from the menu-bar icon, then drag it from Applications to the Bin.

Your clips stay in your library folder (Twitch Archive in your user folder, unless you chose another) until you delete them. To remove everything, also delete ClipVault’s data folder (%LOCALAPPDATA%\ClipVault on Windows, ~/Library/Application Support/ClipVault on a Mac) and the ClipVault entries in your keychain (Credential Manager on Windows, Keychain Access on a Mac).
