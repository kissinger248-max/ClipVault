3. The ClipVault app

ClipVault runs entirely on your computer. You don’t create an account with us, and the app doesn’t send us usage data, crash reports or your library.

On your computer, ClipVault stores your clips and their details, its database, settings and log files in the folders you choose and in its own data folder. Your Twitch sign-in, panel password and any keys you add are stored in your operating system’s keychain. You can delete all of it at any time.

4. Services the app connects to

To work, ClipVault connects to other services directly from your computer. Each has its own privacy policy.

  • Twitch: to find clips, download them, read chat replays of finished streams and read live chat. Twitch sees these requests as coming from your signed-in Twitch account (or from your own developer keys, if you add them).
  • GitHub: ClipVault checks GitHub for a newer version of itself every few hours and downloads updates from there, and its download tool, yt-dlp, checks for its own updates once a day. These requests carry no information about you or your clips. You can switch the update check off in Settings.
  • Hugging Face: the first time ClipVault makes subtitles on your computer, it downloads the free speech model from Hugging Face.

Only if you turn them on:

  • Transcription: if you add a [TRANSCRIPTION PROVIDER, e.g. Groq] key, each clip’s audio is sent to that provider to make subtitles. Without a key, subtitles are made on your computer.
  • AI headlines: if you add an AI key, a clip’s transcript is sent to that provider to suggest a headline.
  • Phone notifications: if you set up ntfy, the text of your summaries and alerts is sent to the ntfy server you use. Anyone who knows your private topic name can read them.

5. Using the panel on your phone

If you allow phones on your Wi-Fi, the control panel is reachable from devices on your local network and is protected by your panel password. It isn’t made reachable from the internet by ClipVault.
